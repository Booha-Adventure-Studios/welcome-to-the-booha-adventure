ブーハー・アドベンチャーへようこそ

ここは、ブーハーの世界の中で、私が生徒に直接話すページです。

This isn't just a page with games on it. It's a learning world that opens a little more every time you move forward.

I built Booha after watching students study English, forget it, study it again, and slowly start to think the problem was them. It wasn't.

The real problem was that English never stayed in one place long enough to feel familiar. Booha gives English a place to live.

Every week, you move through the maze and reach a checkpoint. When you reach it, two things open: your weekly study decks and your weekly game decks. One warms up your brain. The other lets you use what you warmed up.

If you touch both, even a little each day, English stops feeling heavy and starts feeling normal. Less pressure. More momentum.

This is a private system. It is not connected to AI, nothing is recorded outside of your device, and even I cannot track your progress — your data belongs to you and stays on your device unless you choose to export it.

There are three curricula: Pre-Boo, Boo-riculum, and Boo-Continuum. If you're familiar with Eiken, think of it like this:

CurriculumEiken Level
Pre-BooAround Grade 5
Boo-riculumGrade 4 to Grade 2
Boo-ContinuumGrade 1 and above

Most students use Boo-riculum. It's designed to loop and build practical conversational English without locking you into strict levels — because your level is really based on how much you use the language.

Inside the Adventure you'll find three weeks of study decks: this week, last week, and next week. Each week includes 15 vocabulary words, 15 sentences, and 15 questions, all with full audio, along with a hands-free stream for listening and repeating. You can also talk to Uhibon — I'll let you figure that out.

After studying, go to the maze and play the weekly 9 games. The maze is not just a map — it's a connected world with different areas like Karasuki and other hidden spaces, where things begin to appear, change, and unlock over time. The more you explore and complete games, the more the world reveals itself.

Your local memory does not reset, but your weekly game stats do. Every Monday is a fresh start — anyone can begin at any time and fully join the system.

We do not write during lessons. Writing happens at home so class time can be used for speaking and real communication. Every student should keep a diary — each week, write the weekly vocabulary in English and Japanese and bring it to class.

Preschool — under 5 minutes
  • Practice writing their name
  • Trace the alphabet
  • Listen to the question audio
Grades 1–3 — 5 to 10 minutes
  • Write the weekly vocabulary (English + Japanese)
  • Listen to the Study Deck audio
  • Review the sentence cards
  • Practice answering 1–2 speaking questions out loud
Grades 4–6 — 10 to 12 minutes
  • Write the weekly vocabulary (English + Japanese)
  • Review the sentence and question decks
  • Practice saying the weekly answers out loud
  • Keep a short diary (3–4 simple sentences per week)
Junior High — 10 to 15 minutes
  • Write the weekly vocabulary (English + Japanese)
  • Review the Study Deck (vocab → sentences → questions)
  • Say the weekly answers out loud
  • Keep a simple weekly English diary (4–5 sentences)
High School — 12 to 20 minutes
  • Write weekly vocabulary (English + Japanese)
  • Review sentences and questions
  • Say answers out loud — fluency over perfection
  • Keep an English diary (5–8 sentences, including opinions)
  • Use the Game Deck when possible
Adults
  • Come prepared. The goal is to move away from scripts and into real communication.
  • Common habits that block progress: speaking English with Japanese logic, wanting "perfect lines" instead of conversations, using vague words for everything (enjoy, famous, so-so, busy…), having almost no curiosity about English-speaking culture.
  • Adults improve fastest when they can talk about what they think — not just where they went and what they ate.
Study Decks The study decks are the foundation of everything in the Booha Adventure. Nothing else works properly without them. Every week, they always contain vocabulary cards, sentence cards, and question cards — all with full audio.

The vocabulary cards train your ear and eyes to recognize the week's core words quickly. The sentence cards show how those words actually live inside real English. The question cards prepare you to react and answer without freezing, because you've already met the language calmly before the games ask you to use it.

These decks are not meant to be finished once. They are meant to be visited often until the English starts to feel familiar.

Game Decks Each weekly adventure deck includes Tap Match, Say the Word, Word Builder, Eigo Pera Pera, Make the Sentences, and Eigo Adventure.

When the study decks feel familiar, these games feel smooth. When the study decks are skipped, these games feel confusing. The system is honest like that.

Monthly Review Decks In the middle of each month, glowing wooden signs appear on the maze. These unlock the monthly review decks — no audio on purpose. The only way forward is understanding. And sometimes magical trees appear somewhere in the maze, unlocking unique review-style challenges. These are landmarks — they show that your skills have connected enough to unlock something new.

スタディデッキ スタディデッキはブーハーの土台です。ここが弱いと、他がうまく回りません。毎週、必ず「単語カード」「文カード」「質問カード」の3つが入っています。全部フル音声です。

単語カードは、その週の中心になる単語を音と文字の両方で素早く認識できるようにします。文カードは、単語が本当の英語の中でどう使われるかを見せます。質問カードは、ゲームで答えるときに固まらないように先に落ち着いて英語に触れておくための準備です。

1回で終わらせるものではありません。何度も開いて、英語が「見たことある」になっていくのが目的です。

ゲームデッキ 毎週のデッキには、Tap Match、Say the Word、Word Builder、Eigo Pera Pera、Make the Sentences、Eigo Adventure が入っています。

学習デッキが慣れているほど、ゲームはスムーズになります。学習を飛ばすと、ゲームが急に難しく感じます。ブーハーはそこが正直です。

月の復習デッキ 月の真ん中頃になると、迷路のどこかに「光る木の看板」が出てきます。そこに行くと、その月全体の復習デッキが開きます。復習デッキは音声なし。わざとです。学んだものが本当に残っているかをチェックするためです。ときどき「魔法の木」も現れます。これは特別な復習チャレンジが開く場所で、飾りではなく世界のランドマークです。
